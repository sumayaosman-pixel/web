01 — Before the call

A cross-sell call is won before you pick up the phone.

The difference between a call a business owner takes and one they resent is about ten minutes of homework. Trading pattern, what they already use, what they rang about last time.

I do that now, and not because anyone asked me to. Every week I query product adoption across my accounts to find the ones showing a reason to call. Usage tells you what someone is about to need before they say it out loud.

three things I'd want open

How they're tradingVolume by week, not by month. A quiet October after a busy summer is a cash flow conversation, not a bad lead.
What they already useWhich products are switched on, and which were sold and never set up. The second list is where most of the easy value sits.
What they last complained aboutIf the last contact was a problem, that's the first thing I mention. Pretending it didn't happen is how you lose the next twenty minutes.

03 — The call

Four things I do on a call like this.

Not a script. The order, and roughly what I'd say.

01 — Don't open with the product

Open with their year

Owners will tell you about seasonality for five minutes if you let them. "How did the summer actually go for you?" Everything I need is in that answer.

02 — Find the plan, not the pain

What are they trying to do next

A second site, a new freezer, two more staff. Products attach to plans far more easily than they attach to problems.

03 — One thing, costed plainly

Not the whole suite

Say what it costs, what it does, and what the catch is before they ask. A business owner who feels sold to tells the next three people on the high street.

04 — Say when it isn't right

And write down why

"Not this quarter" is information, not rejection. Log the reason and the date, and come back with a better one.
