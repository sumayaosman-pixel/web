01 — The first month

A portfolio is not a list. It is four hundred different years.

Some of those founders are about to raise. Some signed up, did one thing and vanished. Some will churn in ninety days and nobody has noticed yet.

The strategy call is where you find out which is which. Everything after it is just knowing who to ring, and when.

how I'd spend it

01 — Listen first

Ask about the year, not the product

Hiring, raising, a second round, a grant. Their plan decides which product is relevant, not the other way round.

02 — Segment honestly

Not everyone needs a call

Some accounts need a quarterly check-in, some need an email, some need ringing this week. Treating them the same is how 400 becomes unmanageable.

03 — Write it down

CRM the same day

If it is not logged, it did not happen. I keep records clean enough that the next call starts where the last one ended.

04 — Come back with a reason

Never "just checking in"

A no is a date, not a door closing. Note what changed, and ring back when the thing they told you about actually happens.

03 — Triggers

What I already watch for on my own accounts.

Every week I query product adoption and usage. These are the patterns that put an account on my call list.

Trigger

Bought, never switched on

The clearest churn signal there is, and the easiest to fix with one call. It is most of my re-engagement work.

Trigger

Usage climbing fast

Growth is an upsell conversation before it becomes a complaint about limits.

Trigger

Quiet since onboarding

Nobody opens a ticket to say they stopped caring. You only catch it by looking.

Trigger

A date they told me about

A hire, a round, a launch. The best reason to call is the one they gave you last quarter.
